01 / Product opportunity

Make the work of competitive sourcing economically viable.

Context: A buyer handling small, frequent indirect purchasing requests faces many of the same administrative tasks as a strategic sourcing event. Intake clarification, policy routing, supplier discovery, RFQ setup, follow-up, quote normalization and award documentation compete for limited buyer attention.

Customer / primary user

Tactical buyer

Needs a ready-to-review event and a supplier recommendation she can confidently explain.

Governance / enabler

Customer administrator

Needs bounded policy controls, auditable configuration changes and evaluations before publishing.

Economic buyer

Chief Procurement Officer

Needs more spend under management, defensible decisions and credible operational ROI.

Product insight:

The differentiator is not an AI-generated RFQ. It is an end-to-end, governed event that a buyer would otherwise skip—ending in a decision supported by source evidence.

Source basis: your Autonomous Sourcing Agent PRD v2 and leadership review. The customer scenarios and quoted prices below are illustrative, not customer outcomes.

02 / Product discovery

Identify the constraint, not just the repetitive task.

Pain points investigated in the product proposal

Too much work for too little value

Low-value purchases still demand specification authoring, supplier outreach, event setup, quote comparison and recordkeeping. Buyers triage by urgency and spend size.

Demand and data are fragmented

Similar requests are dispersed across teams; catalog, contract, supplier and price history have to be assembled manually.

Supplier responses are hard to compare

Quotes arrive in portals or email, with missing or inconsistent fields. Re-entering information introduces effort and risk.

Decisions lack an evidence trail

When a buyer is asked why a supplier won, the underlying scores, responses, weighting and exception rationale can be difficult to reconstruct.

Product-discovery hypotheses to validate

  • Buyers will run more competitive events if an accurate draft and supplier shortlist already exist inside their current workflow.
  • Clear, quote-linked award explanations—not just a confidence score—will drive trust and repeat use.
  • Customer admins will enable broader coverage when changes can be replay-tested and reviewed before publication.
  • Starting with repeatable indirect categories is safer than beginning with direct materials or strategic contracts.
Evidence boundary

These are hypotheses and product design findings from the PRD and leadership materials, not claims of completed user-research samples or measured production adoption.

03 / Buyer user journey

Earn the buyer's trust at the award moment.

The proposed experience moves from task execution to buyer oversight. It stays within the procurement application and uses conversational intake to reduce context switching.

01

Awareness

Buyer discovers sourcing assistance on a non-catalogue request.

02

First use

Agent proposes category, item description and suppliers based on available history.

03

Trust

Buyer asks, “Why did B beat D?” and sees the price, criteria and source quote.

04

Habit

Routine eligible requests route through the agent; exceptions stay visible.

05

Advocacy

Evidence of reliable awards supports adding users and eligible categories.

Request

“Source 50 ergonomic chairs for Dallas.” The workflow checks contract coverage, policy and category fit.

Buyer gate 1 — requirement

The buyer corrects or confirms the specification and category, rather than drafting a blank event.

Buyer gate 2 — event

The buyer accepts suppliers and event terms before anything is communicated externally.

Evidence-first recommendation

Responses are normalized and scored. A worked example compares a $280/unit offer against a $292/unit offer, with exclusions and delivery differences surfaced.

Buyer gate 3 — award

A named human confirms or overrides the recommendation; the decision and reasoning become part of the audit record.

04 / Proposed AI architecture

Four bounded agents, three product surfaces, one accountable buyer.

Agent 01Intake & Policy

Parse intent, verify permissions and check contracts, catalog, limits and routing.

Agent 02Category & Supplier

Draft from purchase history; recommend qualified suppliers with reasons.

Agent 03Event

Prepare, publish after approval, track responses and normalize offline quotes.

Agent 04Evaluation & Award

Score bids, cite evidence, flag exceptions and prepare a human decision record.

Separation of concerns

Experience layer

Buyer workbench

Conversational request capture, reviewable sourcing drafts, event status and award confirmation.

Control plane

Admin configuration

Typed, bounded thresholds, weights and routing policies; replay evaluations and versioned approvals.

Execution layer

Agent platform

Versioned agent instructions, tools, workflow orchestration, guardrails, logging and per-tenant isolation.

Key governance decision:

Developers define tested agent behavior. Customer administrators configure permitted limits. Buyers choose suppliers and awards. No actor silently substitutes for another.

Fail-safe controls

  • Never infer unsupported supplier facts; trace quote-derived prices and criteria to the response.
  • Pause on low confidence, missing required certifications, conflicting quotes or tool failures.
  • Require three explicit human approval gates at Level 2: specification, launch and award.
  • Version and evaluate changes before broadening the allowed scope.

05 / MVP decisions and trade-offs

Supervised autonomy first. Full automation later—if earned.

In phase 1

What makes the cut

  • Repeatable indirect tail-spend categories with historical context
  • Conversational intake and buyer-editable draft
  • Supplier shortlist and RFQ execution with approvals
  • Portal and emailed quote comparison
  • Explainable ranked recommendation and audit record
  • Bounded configuration and replay evaluations
Deliberately deferred

What is not MVP

  • Automatic supplier award without a buyer
  • Negotiation bots and savings attribution
  • Strategic and direct-material sourcing
  • Contract generation
  • Broad autonomous cross-category rollout
  • Higher autonomy levels without measured evidence

Why Level 2?

The agent can complete the operational steps between approval gates, but a named human approves the specification, event launch and award. This reflects the product's trust and auditability goals, while avoiding exaggerated claims of fully autonomous purchasing.

Key trade-off

Retaining mandatory human approvals limits maximum automation speed, but narrows error exposure, keeps accountability explicit and gives customers a testable path to broader autonomy.

06 / Evaluation and measurement

Measure sourcing outcomes alongside evidence quality and trust.

North star · proposedTail spend competitively sourced per active buyer

Per quarter, among eligible requests; avoids counting empty AI activity as business value.

TrustAward acceptance + reasoned overrides

Track evidence drill-down, override causes and time-to-decision together to identify automation bias.

EfficiencyBuyer touches per event

Measure elapsed need-to-live-event time and events closed by declared criteria.

QualityCategory and quote accuracy

Monitor correction rates, supplier-list edits, extracted quote quality and policy exceptions.

Proposed eval suite

Replay evals

Re-run recent sourcing cases under changed limits or scoring weights; reveal which decisions would change.

Scenario evals

Challenge cold starts, no responses, unqualified suppliers, conflicting bids and emailed quote extraction.

Capability regressions

Test versioned agent instructions, tool behavior, policy boundaries, refusals and trace completeness before rollout.

Metrics and evaluation methods are proposed. No achieved production coverage, ROI or savings are asserted.
