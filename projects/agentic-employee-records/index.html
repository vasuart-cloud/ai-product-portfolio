Case study 02 / HR compliance AI

Agentic Employee Records.

A governed AI experience for detecting employee-record gaps, explaining the evidence, preparing human-approved remediation and supporting audit readiness.

01 / Product thesis

Employee-record management is an evidence problem, not simply a storage problem.

Product vision: an AI-governed compliance and evidence layer for high-churn workforces. Instead of replacing an HR management system, the proposed product detects gaps across existing records, explains them using policy and source evidence, prepares human-reviewed remediation and captures defensible audit history.

Primary user

HR Operations Manager

Works through employee-file gaps, document follow-ups, expiries, exceptions and audit preparation.

Governance user

HR Compliance Admin

Owns rule versions, policy thresholds, confidence review and separation of duties.

Economic buyer

VP HR / CHRO

Needs defensible evidence of compliance posture, operational efficiency and risk reduction.

Product thesis:

The pivotal adoption moment is when HR asks “Why was this file flagged?” and receives the requirement, evidence source, applicable policy version and confidence together.

02 / Problem discovery

Start with high-frequency gaps and expensive audit friction.

The product review frames the opportunity in healthcare, staffing, manufacturing, logistics, BPO and other high-churn environments, where files can be scattered across HCM systems, content repositories and email.

Records exist, but are hard to defend

Missing or expired certificates, right-to-work documents, policy acknowledgements and training records become audit issues when the evidence and rule cannot be tied together.

Manual reviews create a reactive queue

HR teams hunt across systems, contact employees repeatedly and prioritize findings without a single explanation or SLA view.

Governance is difficult to reproduce

When policy configurations change, administrators need to know which rule version generated which finding and whether the change breaks previous cases.

Buyers struggle to quantify the value

CHROs need a credible reduction in time-to-resolution and audit-preparation work, not simply more AI-generated alerts.

Proposed validation work

  • Interview 20–30 HR/compliance stakeholders in high-churn sectors and shadow five file-review workflows.
  • Baseline missing-file volume, evidence-retrieval effort, manual follow-ups and audit-preparation time.
  • Start with two or three painful document types; confirm that explainability and remediation create paid urgency.
  • Seek two to five paid design partners before committing to a broad rollout.

Interview counts, design-partner numbers and thresholds are planned validation activities described in the product presentation—not completed research or signed customers.

A compliance finding appears for an employee record, with severity and evidence status.

Explain

The agent drafts a remediation task or message; a human reviews before outreach.

04 / Proposed AI architecture

Deterministic orchestration around bounded agents.

The design favors a controlled sequence with known inputs, outputs and audit events over an unrestricted agent that can invent or execute compliance actions without review.

Agent 01Ingestion

Connect, classify and map documents to employee evidence.

Agent 02Compliance

Compare evidence with policy and rule versions; flag severity and confidence.

Agent 03Remediation

Draft requests, route human approvals and manage escalation SLAs.

Agent 04Audit & Retrieval

Answer “why,” link source evidence and assemble the decision record.

Cross-cutting design requirements

Rule and source grounding

Every finding needs an identifiable source document, policy reference, confidence signal and rule version.

Human accountability

HR confirms findings and approves outbound communications or closure. The agent assists; it does not silently take responsibility.

Governance and isolation

Role-based access, a separate author/approver flow, PII guardrails and tested configuration changes.

Observability

Track false positives, overrides, agent errors, policy impact, action history and SLA breaches.

Architecture principle:

Trigger → retrieve evidence → classify → apply versioned policy → create finding → require approval → remediate → verify → close. Every transition emits traceable evidence.

05 / MVP decisions and product roadmap

Prove governed remediation on a narrow set of records.

Proposed alpha · months 0–3

Initial wedge

  • Employee dossier and file-health view
  • Selected I-9, policy acknowledgement and certification gaps
  • “Why flagged?” evidence and confidence panel
  • Four-agent proposed orchestration
  • Human approvals, audit log and bounded remediation
Later phases / intentionally deferred

Do not over-build yet

  • Broad global regulatory rule library
  • Fully autonomous closure or employee outreach
  • Full document retention / records replacement
  • Complex case management and multi-country coverage
  • Marketplace, bulk features and wider integrations before trust is demonstrated

Why this sequence?

Document repositories already exist. The differentiated value must come from fewer unresolved gaps, faster reviews, reliable evidence, and a workflow HR is willing to trust. Phased investment should depend on those outcomes and buyer willingness to pay.

06 / Evaluation strategy and success criteria

Separate AI activity from verified compliance outcomes.

North star · proposedVerified complete employee files within policy SLA

Completeness must be supported by evidence and governed closure, not simply alert generation.

TrustFinding confirmation and override reasons

Track “Why?” drill-down, accepted / dismissed findings and whether reviewers understand the evidence.

QualityClassification and policy accuracy

Test false positives, false negatives, missing sources and rule-version regression behavior.

Efficiency and adoptionTime to resolve and weekly active HR users

Measure touches per gap, median closure time, SLAs and sustained repeat use.

Proposed evaluation framework

  1. Ground truth: build a representative, access-controlled set of records labelled by expert reviewers and policy version.
  2. Replay: test changes to rules or thresholds against prior findings before publish.
  3. Confidence gates: route ambiguous or missing evidence for human inspection rather than auto-closure.
  4. Pilot comparison: baseline gap handling and audit prep, then measure real-world trust and efficiency against the original workflow.
  5. Decision gate: pursue paid pilots and expand only when quality, trust, ROI and security evidence justify it.
Measurement status:

The product deck suggests pilot success thresholds, including weekly adoption and faster gap resolution. These are targets to be tested, not results already realized.
