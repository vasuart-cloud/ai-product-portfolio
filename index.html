Agentic Employee Records

Product vision: an AI-governed employee-record compliance and evidence automation layer for high-churn, labor-heavy workforces. Instead of another document repository, the concept focuses on detecting file gaps, explaining why they matter, guiding human-reviewed remediation, and making audit evidence defensible.

Business problem

Employee evidence is scattered across HRMS, document stores, and email. Manual reviews, missed expiries, unclear rule versions, and audit preparation create avoidable operational risk.

Primary personas

HR Operations Managers need speed; Compliance Administrators need rules, approvals, and traceability; VPs of HR and CHROs need audit-readiness and ROI evidence.

My contribution

Developed the product review and prototype experience, including problem framing, personas, solution strategy, agent roles, governance, phased roadmap, metrics, validation and GTM hypotheses.

Proposed MVP architecture · four bounded agents

01
Ingestion Agent

Connect to document sources, classify records, and map supporting evidence to employee files.

02
Compliance Agent

Evaluate completeness against policies; surface confidence, severity, and applicable rule versions.

03
Remediation Agent

Draft follow-ups and tasks, route approval, and track remediation SLAs and exceptions.

04
Audit Agent

Explain why records were flagged and compile source links and action history for reviews.

Product principles & validation approach

Explain before acting

Expose the source, rule, confidence, and evidence behind each finding. Maintain human approval before external communication or closure.

Earn trust through metrics

Measure confirmed findings, false positives, time to resolve, SLA breaches, and verified complete employee files.

Validate before scaling

Product-review recommendation: pursue 2–5 paid design partners and test trust, operational ROI, and buyer willingness to pay before full-scale development.

Project status: The live URL opens an interactive prototype. The agent architecture, integrations, metrics, roadmap, pricing, and GTM details in the presentation are product proposals or validation targets—not claims that those capabilities are production-deployed or that those targets have been achieved.

Product concept & prototype exploration

Autonomous Sourcing Agent

Making competitive sourcing practical for low-value, high-volume purchases — by automating sourcing administration while keeping procurement decisions and controls with people.

01 · CaptureUnderstand the needConversational intake and policy check
02 · DraftPrepare the eventPast purchase context and suppliers
03 · ExecuteRun sourcingPublish, collect and normalize quotes
04 · DecideExplain the awardRanked options with cited evidence
Tail-spend sourcingMulti-agent orchestrationHuman-in-the-loopProcurement AIAI governance

Product opportunity: Many small purchasing requests never go through a competitive sourcing event. They require much of the same manual effort as larger purchases, so procurement teams prioritize high-value events and often accept familiar suppliers and unchallenged prices. This concept turns the routine steps of sourcing into an agent-led workflow, while a buyer still approves the decision.

The pain point

Buyers chase missing requirements, re-enter specifications, find suppliers, create RFQs, follow up on responses and manually compare inconsistent quotes. Fragmented demand and undocumented decisions compound the problem.

Who it serves

Tactical buyers want to source more requests with fewer touches. Customer administrators need safe configuration and oversight. Procurement leaders need more spend under management and defensible awards.

My product contribution

Developed the product concept, problem framing, buyer journeys, requirements, agent responsibilities, approval model, evaluation strategy, proposed roadmap and success metrics.

Proposed solution · four specialized agents

01
Intake & Policy Agent

Interprets a conversational request, fills gaps and checks contracts, catalog options and routing policies.

02
Category & Supplier Agent

Drafts specifications from relevant purchase history, identifies categories and recommends suppliers with reasons.

03
Event Agent

Prepares and monitors sourcing events, follows up for bids, and brings emailed quotes into one comparison.

04
Evaluation & Award Agent

Compares bids against approved criteria and produces an explainable, evidence-linked award recommendation.

Designed for supervised autonomy · three human approval gates

GATE 1
Confirm the requirement

The buyer reviews the item description and category before proceeding.

GATE 2
Authorize the event

The buyer approves suppliers and event details before outreach.

GATE 3
Make the award

A named human confirms or overrides the recommendation with a reason.

Evidence over black-box AI

Show why one supplier outranks another, with traceable quotes, scoring criteria, exclusions and a recorded human decision — not an unsupported recommendation.

Customer-controlled guardrails

Use defined spend thresholds, scoring weights, supplier policies and approval routing. Test proposed changes with replay and scenario evaluations before publication.

A deliberately narrow MVP

Start with repeatable indirect tail-spend categories. Keep automated awards, negotiation and direct strategic sourcing out of the initial supervised scope.

How I would measure product success

North-star metric: competitively sourced tail spend per active buyer. Supporting signals: buyer effort per event, request-to-event time, repeat usage, quote coverage, award acceptance and overrides, and how often buyers inspect supporting evidence.
